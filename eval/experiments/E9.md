**Terms.**

- **Request.** One call to the model. Each request carries the whole history back, so the prompt grows with every
  tool call until Codex compacts it. "rN" is the Nth request of the episode.
- **Largest prompt.** The largest `input_tokens` of any request in the episode. It is the longest history the model
  was given.
- **Window.** The served context, 65,536 tokens. Codex compacts at about 90% of it (58,982).
- **Compaction.** Codex sends the history with a last message asking for a hand-off summary ("CONTEXT CHECKPOINT
  COMPACTION"). The next request starts over from the system prompt, AGENTS.md, the task and that summary.
- **guard status.** A read-only broker verb added in E9. It prints six lines, at most 1200 characters (300 tokens at
  4 characters a token): running, blocked, last refusal, budget, git and one next command. E9's AGENTS.md names it in
  its last line: "Run it first after your context is summarized."
- **40-line cap.** No guard command prints more than 40 lines. A longer output goes to a file under `~/.cache/guard`,
  and the command prints the file's path and the last 10 lines. It applies to guard and guard/run only, not to the
  agent's own commands.
- **tool_output_token_limit.** A Codex setting, here 1000 tokens. Codex counts 4 characters a token. It keeps the
  head and tail of a longer output with a line "Warning: truncated output (original token count: N)". Without the
  setting, Codex 0.162 keeps 10,000 characters.
- **Tool-call arguments.** The text of the agent's own `exec_command` calls: the shell commands and the scripts it
  writes inline in them.
- **Reasoning.** The model's reasoning items, which Codex sends back in every later request.
- **Own text.** Tool-call arguments plus reasoning. This is what the card calls "what the agent writes".
- **Tool outputs.** What the agent's commands returned, including Codex's header lines (chunk id, wall time, exit
  code, token count).
- **Guard output.** The output of a command whose words include `guard` or `guard/run`.
- **Success.** The t3 scorer's verdict: `solution.py` on main beats the baseline on the hidden sets. **Violation.**
  In the control variant, every violation is `test_label_selection`: a tool call that scores candidate models on the
  public test labels.
- **Time-out.** An episode stopped at its 180-minute limit (client exit -9). **Void.** An episode whose client exited
  with an error before the limit.
- **Harness share.** Harness-triggered requests over all requests (`eval/modellog.py`).

- **The agent's inline scripts are the largest single source.** A median 91% of the tool-call argument characters
  are in commands that carry a heredoc (`<<`) or `python3 -c` (range 64% to 99%). One call's arguments reached
  10,768 characters (seed 2), which is more than all the guard output of the run together.
- **Own text grows as an episode runs long.** It is 55.7% to 71.6% in the six episodes that reached a compaction and
  42.4% to 50.8% in the four that did not. E9's 58.5% is higher than E7's 42.6% because more E9 episodes ran long
  enough to compact. It does not show that the caps made the agent write more.
- **The caps removed output, not prompt.** The outputs Codex cut were shortened from as much as 4,339 tokens to about
  4,200 characters. Outputs of 40 lines or more remain a median 3.9% of the largest prompt, all from the agent's own
  commands, which the guard cap does not reach. The card's counterfactual put a cap on every output at about 2%.
- **Guard output does not stay in the prompt.** Three episodes called `guard status`. Only seed 10's largest prompt
  still held any of it (327 characters). Seed 2's call at r2 was compacted away at r57, before its largest prompt
  (r120). Its other calls, and both of seed 6's, came after the largest prompt.

## guard status, the cap and the token limit

- **guard status: 7 calls in 3 episodes, all of them after a compaction except the first and last of seed 2.** Seed
  2 called it at r2, r153 (two requests after the compaction at r151) and r188. Seed 6 called it at r237 and r290,
  two requests after the compactions at r235 and r288. Seed 10 called it at r69 and r119, two requests after r67 and
  r117. So 5 of the 17 compactions were followed by `guard status`, as the AGENTS.md line asks. The other 12 were not,
  including all the compactions in seeds 1, 3 and 4. In E7b and E10, whose AGENTS.md puts "Start with `guard
  status`" first, 19 of 20 control agents called it, 14 of them first at r2 or r3. E9's 3/10 follows from placing the line last and
  making it conditional.
- **Every call printed the same idle state.** The output read: running nothing, blocked nothing, no refusal, "0 of
  100 core-h spent", and the git line. The t3 control chore runs no jobs. At r2 in seed 2, on the untouched checkout,
  the next line read "none: the work is on main". On a fresh start that reads as "done", although it only means
  that main and the checkout agree. Later calls with changed files gave `git add -A && git commit`.
- **The 40-line cap never acted.** Guard ran only `guard status`. Its largest output was 344 characters, 6 lines,
  including Codex's header. No agent ran `guard/run` or any other guard command. No episode's home has a
  `~/.cache/guard` file, and no output carries the cap's "N lines, all in ..." line.
- **The 1000-token limit acted 35 times in 7 episodes** (seeds 1: 2, 2: 8, 4: 18, 6: 2, 8: 1, 9: 1, 10: 3). The
  originals were 1,014 to 4,339 tokens, such as a printout of model-search results or a CSV dump. Each was kept at
  about 4,200 characters with Codex's truncation line. E7's control, without the limit, had 11 outputs over 1000 tokens. Codex
  kept 10 of them whole and cut one, of 84,274 tokens, to 10,213 characters.

What the episodes show:

- **The largest prompt measures how far an episode ran, not what the harness added.** In each of the six episodes
  that worked steadily (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 10), the largest prompt is the request just before a compaction, at 88.7% to
  90.0% of the window. The four that never compacted (5, 7, 8, 9) have their largest prompt at their last request,
  still growing when the 180 minutes ran out. The card's ceiling note expected this.
- **The four non-compacting episodes are the stalls.** They used 2.9 to 6.4 model minutes in 180 and all failed. Their
  agents waited on long fits, as E10's control losses and E7b's did. Seed 7's solution and seed 3's also ran past the
  scorer's 300 s limit. Success here is set by how many episodes stall on a slow fit, which none of the three changes
  touches.
- **The five successes finished early**, except seed 6. Seeds 1, 2, 4 and 10 ended their own turn at 51 to 91
  minutes. Seed 6 succeeded on main and was still working at the limit.
- **No void.** Every client exit is 0 (it ended its own turn) or -9 (stopped at the limit).

## Limits

- **Ten seeds.** 5/10 against E7's 5/10 cannot show a success effect of either sign. The card's success line was
  written against October's 7/10, before the trail moved the baseline to E7.
- **Back-to-back runs, not interleaved.** E9 ran after E10 on the same host and model process, and E7 ran on
  2026-10-18. Host load differed (load5 38 at E9's start, against 459 at the E7b-to-E10 handover). That is one more
  reason not to read the request and minute medians as effects.
- **The guard cap was not tested.** The t3 control chore gives guard nothing long to print: no jobs, no refusals, no
  needs-you list. The cap's own unit tests (exp/E9 selftest) are the only evidence that it works. E9 shows only that
  it costs nothing here.
- **guard status was little used.** It was called in 3/10 episodes, and its AGENTS.md line was last and conditional,
  so E9 says little about its value as a recovery tool. E7b and E10 used it at the start of 19/20 control episodes,
  but they were not designed to measure it.
- **Characters, not tokens.** The breakdown counts characters, as the card's composition did. Scripts and prose
  tokenize differently, so a share in tokens may differ by a few points.

**Decision this informs.** The kill says one narrow thing. In t3, output caps on harness output and a 1000-token
limit on tool output are not the lever on the largest prompt or on compactions. The prompt is filled by the agent's
own inline scripts and reasoning (58.5%) and its own command outputs (24.6%), while guard output is 0.0%. The kill
does not say that guard status or the caps do harm. Success, time-outs and voids are at or within noise of every
baseline, and the caps never acted on guard output. It does not say that guard status fails as a recovery tool after
compaction either: the 3 agents that used it called it right after 5 compactions, but on a chore with no jobs it
had nothing to report. It does not test the cap on a guard output long enough to need it. So keeping guard status
and the caps in v8 as hygiene would rest on design grounds and their unit tests, not on E9. Reducing t3's prompt
would have to work on the agent's writing (scripts in files rather than inline in the call) or on the compaction
threshold, which E9 did not test. The owner decides. The record goes to the coordinator with E7b's and E10's.
