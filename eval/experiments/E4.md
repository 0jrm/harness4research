**Verdict: killed.** Median poll requests 45.5, over the kill line of 30 (baseline 47). Success fell from 7/10 to 2/10, and 8 of 10 episodes ran out their 180 minutes. Decision: not merged; this record goes to the owner. **Terms.** A poll is a request whose new tool calls include `write_stdin` with empty `chars` (or `exec_command` with an empty `cmd`). `yield_time_ms` is how long that call waits for output; it returns early when the process exits. A job is one `exec_command` that is still running when its first call returns, so Codex hands back a session id to poll.

**What happened.** `guard wait` ran in one episode only: 79717b4d9a9d (seed 3), 10 calls, all `guard wait -- python3 evaluate.py`. That episode succeeded. The other nine never called it. They did follow the last sentence of the line. Polls of 300000 ms or more appear in 6 of 10 E4 episodes and in 1 of 10 October episodes. f0efab769297 polled at 600000 ms 17 times; the raised Codex cap let those run the full 600 s. The poll count stayed where it was, so the kill fired.

Episode e49d548e5db1 (seed 4) shows the pattern. Requests 35 to 64 are polls with `yield_time_ms` 300000 on three jobs in turn: sessions 91055 (opened at request 34), 24417 (request 40) and 40702 (request 50). Each is a `python3 -c` cross-validated GradientBoostingRegressor grid. Each poll returned after exactly 300 s with "Process running" and 0 tokens of output: the script's stdout goes to a pipe and is block-buffered, so nothing shows until it exits. When a job did end during a poll, the poll came back early (request 39's poll returned at 164 s with the grid results, and request 64's after 13 s). The three jobs ran 23, 44 and 66 minutes. A fourth, session 95511 (request 65), was still running when the episode was killed at 180 minutes. The jobs were not slowed by a crowded machine. `time` output in 12c4ce7102fd shows real time equal to user time (for example 11m50s real, 11m43s user). The agent beat the baseline at request 33 (RMSE 1.363 against 1.598) and never wrote `results.md` or pushed.

In October, the episodes that succeeded mostly left long jobs running and moved on to another attempt. 1a0b859a77e3 left 28 jobs that way and pushed at minute 36; cb70a91021c0 left 9 and 8a524f214a38 left 7. Only one October episode, 2f0cb8e32f0d, timed out by sitting out its jobs (34 and 57 minutes, one more running at the end). The other two October time-outs, 4b2e341879d7 and 06d4e91e9870, ended with `pkill -f python3`, which killed the model relay (fixed in #137, which E4 includes).

**The BrokenPipeError is harmless and old.** Every logged reply that ends "gateway error: BrokenPipeError" holds a full `response.completed` event before the error: 116 of 116 in 79717b4d9a9d, 13 of 13 in e49d548e5db1, and so on in every episode. Codex closes the stream as soon as it reads `response.completed`, and the gateway's last write (the closing chunk) then hits a closed socket (`lib/gateway.py`, `forward`). No request repeats its predecessor's input, so no reply was retried or lost. The October episodes show the same error at the same rate (31 of 112 in 2f0cb8e32f0d, 92 of 289 in 3c5fddbccaa2). The 300 s is not a timeout anywhere. It is the agent's own `yield_time_ms`, and each poll's output reports "Wall time: 300.00 seconds". Model requests take about 1 s.

**Root cause.** The line asked for two things. Agents dropped `guard wait` and kept "wait once with the longest wait the tool allows". On a job that prints nothing until it ends, that turns a 30 s glance into five or ten minutes of commitment. The agent gets no sign that a grid search is too slow, so it rarely stops one. `guard wait` itself worked in the one episode that used it. It cannot cut polls on its own anyway: `exec_command` returns within 30 s whatever runs under it, so every wait longer than that is a `write_stdin` poll.

**Limits.** Ten seeds. E4's base (aa7d6a9) includes #133, which changed the t3 prompt after the October run: it names numpy, pandas, scikit-learn and scipy, and asks for a push as soon as the baseline is beaten. So the cell differs from the baseline by more than the change under test. Every E4 episode used pandas and gradient boosting, while several October ones worked with the csv module and Ridge. Some of the time lost may come from that. The waited-out and left-running counts come from tool-call logs. A job counts as waited out when a poll saw it exit. It counts as left running when it was never seen to exit and the agent stopped polling it more than 10 minutes before the end.

**Decision.** Not merged; the numbers go to the owner. A retry would change three things. Drop the "longest wait the tool allows" sentence and keep Codex's cap at 300000. Have `guard wait` run its command with `PYTHONUNBUFFERED=1` and return every 5 minutes with the last 20 lines, so each wait shows progress and gives the agent a turn to stop a slow job. Compare against a control cell on the same base commit (E7's control arm, or a no-change rerun), not against October.
